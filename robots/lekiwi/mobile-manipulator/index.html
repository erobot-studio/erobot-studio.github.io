The 8-week journey

Every week, the robot can do more than the week before.

  • Week 1Build the arm

    Assemble the 6-joint robot arm from real servos and frames; first power-on.

  • Week 2Calibrate & teleoperate

    Calibrate every joint, then drive the arm with a leader arm; precision pick challenges.

  • Week 3Build the mobile base

    Omniwheels, battery, and the Raspberry Pi brain — the arm gets wheels.

  • Week 4Wake it up

    Connect over Wi-Fi, see the world through the robot's cameras, drive the whole robot.

  • Week 5Pick & place in Python

    Scripted motion plus computer vision: the robot finds and grabs objects on its own.

  • Week 6Autonomous navigation

    The robot drives itself: line following and camera-guided docking — and students learn why closed-loop control beats dead reckoning.

  • Week 7Mission integration + AI training

    Chain navigation and manipulation into one mission; record demonstrations and launch neural network training with LeRobot.

  • Week 8Fetch Quest — demo day

    Families watch each team run a scored autonomous mission with the robot they built, plus the reveal of the AI trained on their own demonstrations.

Demo day

Fetch Quest: 90 minutes your kid won't forget.

The robot starts in its square, follows a course to the Supply Depot, docks by camera, picks up the cargo, and delivers it home — scored feat by feat, with autonomy earning full points and remote assists earning half. Every student narrates part of their team's run. No team can fail; every robot runs.

Program FAQ

About this program

What exactly is “training the robot with AI”?

Students record examples of a task by physically demonstrating it, then train a neural network that maps what the robot's cameras see to what the human did — a technique called imitation learning. It's the same approach used by leading robotics labs today, running on the open-source LeRobot framework from Hugging Face.

Is 12–18 too wide a range for one class?

Cohorts are grouped by age and experience at enrollment — a cohort is at most 4 students, so we never mix a 12-year-old beginner with 17-year-old programmers. Younger students get scaffolded code; older students write more from scratch and take on stretch goals every week.

My kid is under 12 — what are the options?

The build and Python work here is designed for 12+. Drone Explorers (ages 8–11) is in development — join the interest list and we'll let you know first. From age 11, the SO-ARM101 AI Arm Lab is also coming soon; high schoolers can also look at the RaceCar Self-Driving Lab (14–18).

What does a typical session look like?

Every session follows the same rhythm: a hook where the robot does something new, a 15-minute concept lesson (just the one idea the lab needs), 50 minutes of hands-on lab work in pairs, and a team challenge with a stand-up demo — so every week ends with the robot visibly doing something it couldn't do before.
